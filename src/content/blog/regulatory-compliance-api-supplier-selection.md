Perhaps one of the single most crucial decisions for a [**pharma company**](/) can be the selection of an API supplier. This, by itself, cannot be viewed as a ‘simple commodity purchase’, as it cannot escape from product quality and impacts, on the basis of the characteristics and requirements of the drug product of quality, consistency, control and tractability from within a controlled manufacturing processes and systems. Quality, in consequence for this consideration has come to have a major effect as to the decision Making.

By pharmaceutical companies, quality has become a key element, coupled now with aspects of: manufacturing capability; supported by documented evidence; the suppliers regulatory track;; history, and supply integrity and also product quality management systems.

The fundamental criteria for this evaluation have been provided by aspects of regulation and ICH Q7, which gives GMP criteria relevant to [**API manufacture**](/), and guides the alignment of these systems and factors to address API quality attributes and ensure suitability, and an agreed level of quality, and purity criteria as part. The API supplier who offers the product at, for instance, the cheapest price, should not be favoured as a favoured partner if they cannot provide a consistent, guaranteed quality product and appropriate commercial and regulatory back up when needed.

## **What Does Regulatory Compliance Mean for an API Supplier?**

Regulatory Compliance (RC) means abiding by the laws, regulations, standards and quality guidelines applicable to pharmaceutical manufacture and supply chain activities. For an API manufacturers this can include starting materials receipt and handling, their own production, quality control and product specification, packaging, warehousing, shipping etc.

ICH Q7 clearly specifies the elements that relate to these areas under its GMP guidelines.

RC shouldn’t just be a certificate on the wall;it requires established written procedures and process documentation, qualified staff, adequate and controlled process infrastructure, effective testing, quality investigations and quality oversight, and appropriate batch and supply chain tracking etc. However the actual RC will differ greatly for a drug on a European market verses North American market,for use in an ‘essential use drug versus novel biological,’ or for a API used solely in veterinary drugs and so the buyer should tailor their check against their own target market, use and quality parameters.

The FDA Q7 guidance recommends that the process of supplier approval include verification that the manufacturer is capable of providing material of the required specifications on a reproducible and consistent basis and lists annual review of the reliability and Certificates of Analysis of suppliers.

This makes obvious why supplier qualification should be based on evidence, not on the marketing efforts of the supplier.

## **Certifications Are Important—but Not the Whole Story**

While accreditations, certifications, audit history and approval may offer insights into the capabilities and reliability of a potential supplier, Buyers must confirm the scope and currency of these qualifications, as well as the credentials of issuing agencies and their relevance to the products and services offered. A simple certificate is not sufficient without qualification of the supplier. Companies involved in Pharmaceuticals must review the entire value chain – including manufacturing controls, quality systems, product testing, record keeping, regulatory record and the past performance of the supplier. This becomes particularly significant when evaluating suppliers from different regions of the world, serving varied global regulatory environments.

## Product Specifications and Quality Standards

There should be clear and appropriate product specifications. “Product specifications” should reflect the product, including suitable references to pharmacopoeias (e.g. USP, BP, EP, IP or equivalent) where appropriate.

“What the specification entails may relate to the API’s identification, assay, related substances and impurities (organic and inorganic), residual solvents, water or moisture, and physical parameters.

There may also be microbiological criteria where the API may not meet standard chemical specifications.” The appropriate level of specification will depend on both the product, the particular client and the regulatory market involved. There is existing EMA guidance relating to active substances: the Q&A document for ‘Active substances used in medicinal products’ contains detailed recommendations for, among others, control, the manufacturing process and specifications and the nature of an Active Substance’s specification.

## Regulatory Compliance for Global API Sourcing

Global sourcing is yet another complicating factor. A supplier may be called upon to serve customers whose activities would fall under several varied systems of regulation. US FDA would govern [**active ingredients**](/products/) being imported to US as part of pharmaceutical supplies (and would likely call on regulations concerning foreign establishment practices and drug listings) while the similar pharmaceutical ingredients landing on shores in Europe must adhere to European regulatory standards and European expectations as to active ingredient quality standards.

This brings into play a critical and logical question an international buyer must pose to his potential supplier: “Can this supplier meet my supply needs based on standards imposed by my market?”

Answers to this should rely on evidence, not estimation.

## Red Flags When Selecting an API Supplier

A few more things to investigate more thoroughly prior to accepting a supplier: IncompleteDocumentation Vague specifications or certificates and/or incomplete/conflicting data makes qualifying the supplier problematic. Non-verifiable Certification Appropriate supporting documentation must be available from the supplier regarding any certifications or claims of adherence to any regulatory guidance. Unclear Origin Of Manufacturing The buyer should know who manufactures the API itself rather than having any intermediary or distributors present themselves as manufacturers (especially for third part purchased materials).

Bad Quality Communication slow and or lack of responsiveness to technical queries will come back to haunt later on in life.

Mysterious Quality Inconsistency Variations from batch-to-batch should never be dismissed but instead followed-up on. Poor traceability If they can’t give me the right information in support of my traceability of my received material then they should be dropped immediately.

## FAQs:

Why is regulatory compliance important when selecting an API supplier?

Regulatory compliance helps pharmaceutical companies evaluate whether an API supplier has appropriate systems for manufacturing, testing, documenting, and controlling pharmaceutical ingredients.

What should I check before approving an API supplier?

Review manufacturing capability, GMP systems, specifications, analytical testing, documentation, supplier audit results, regulatory information, traceability, supply reliability, and technical support.

Is a GMP certificate enough to qualify an API supplier?

No. A certificate can be useful evidence, but supplier qualification should also consider the certificate’s scope and validity, manufacturing practices, product quality, documentation, audit findings, and ongoing performance.

Why is a Certificate of Analysis important?

A Certificate of Analysis provides batch-specific analytical information and helps customers evaluate whether supplied material meets established specifications. Its reliability should also be appropriately assessed.

Should API suppliers be audited?

An audit can be an important part of supplier qualification, particularly when the API is critical or the supplier’s manufacturing and quality systems require direct assessment.

Do API requirements differ between countries?

Yes. Regulatory and documentation requirements can vary by market. For example, APIs imported into the United States are subject to applicable FDA requirements, while European markets have their own regulatory frameworks.

What makes a reliable pharmaceutical API supplier?

A reliable supplier combines appropriate quality systems, consistent products, proper documentation, regulatory awareness, dependable supply, effective communication, and the ability to meet customer-specific requirements.

Can compliance reduce API supply-chain risks?

Yes. Strong supplier qualification and quality systems can help identify potential quality and regulatory risks earlier, although they cannot eliminate every possible supply-chain disruption.
