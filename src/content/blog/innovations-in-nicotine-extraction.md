## Traditional Nicotine Extraction Methods

Historically, nicotine was extracted using basic techniques such as:

### 1. Solvent Extraction

Early methods used solvents like ethanol or kerosene to dissolve nicotine from tobacco leaves. Though effective, solvent residues and impurities were a major concern.

### 2. Steam Distillation

This technique relied on high-temperature steam to release nicotine. However, it often degraded the compound, affecting its potency and introducing unwanted by-products.

### 3. Acid-Base Extraction

Widely used in the past, this method involved acidifying tobacco material to extract nicotine, then neutralizing it with a base. Though relatively simple, it was inefficient at achieving high purity levels.

These methods provided the foundation for **pure nicotine production** but left much room for improvement.

## Latest Innovations in Nicotine Extraction

Modern extraction methods focus on maximizing purity, yield, and potency while minimizing environmental impact. Key innovations include:

### 1. Supercritical Fluid Extraction (SFE)

- Uses supercritical CO2 as a solvent.
- Operates at controlled temperatures and pressures.
- Produces extremely pure nicotine without residual solvents.
- Environmentally sustainable.

### 2. Cryogenic Extraction

- Low-temperature extraction preserves nicotine integrity.
- Reduces thermal degradation.
- Enhances overall potency and purity.

### 3. Membrane Separation Technology

- Separates nicotine molecules based on size and charge.
- Offers high selectivity for pure nicotine.
- Lowers energy usage compared to traditional methods.

### 4. Enzyme-Assisted Extraction

- Uses biological catalysts to facilitate nicotine release from tobacco cells.
- Reduces need for harsh chemicals.
- Improves yield and bioactivity of nicotine.

These **nicotine extraction methods** are rapidly becoming the gold standard for companies aiming for **nicotine potency enhancement**.
