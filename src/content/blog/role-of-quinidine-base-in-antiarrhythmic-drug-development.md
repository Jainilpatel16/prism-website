Quinidine Base has been established in cardiovascular pharmacology for many years and represents a significant pharmaceutical ingredient for defining and formulating antiarrhythmic treatments. Having originally been used in the treatment of some types of arrhythmias, as a Class IA agent, influencing heart electricity, quinidine has remained useful throughout a dramatically developed modern approach to treatments and has continued to be of value to the formulation sciences, pharmaceutical research and work into ion channel activity in the heart. The drive of new antiarrhythmic treatments is for superior rhythm control, better safety and selectivity as well as efficient and palatable pharmaceutical use and it is within this environment that [**Quinidine Base**](/api_bulk_drugs/quinidine-base/) API can be an important reference compound.

## **Understanding Quinidine Base**

## **What Is Quinidine Base?**

Quinidine is stereoisomer of quinine and derived from the family of **quinoline alkaloid**. Quinidine Base is the free-base of the active pharmaceutical ingredient and can be serve as intermediate or starting material for specific formulations or drug forms to design.

Quinidine, as with established pharmacology, it can be explored the mechanism to do investigation regarding cardiac electrical conductivity and rhythmic activities.

## **Quinidine and Cardiac Ion Channels**

Quinidine exerts the majority of its actions upon cardiac ion channels essential for the conduction and initiation of action potentials. Specifically, sodium channel blocking properties, in addition to influences upon the potassium current responsible for cardiac conduction and repolarization, have defined quinidine to be central to the development of antiarrhythmic therapies and their assessment.
