A chemical substance in cigarettes, cigars, and electronic cigarettes, [**nicotine**](/how-long-does-nicotine-stay-in-the-system/) is considered a powerful angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor and is highly addictive. Nicotine is taken into the bloodstream and has impact on the brain and body resulting to stimulation signification such as increased heart rate and better mood. But how long does nicotine take to leave the body once it has been ingested? This is important for anyone in the process of quitting, or anyone who is worried about the long-term effects of nicotine on the body.

Here, you’ll learn how the substance is metabolised and the period it remains in the human system as well as the factors that influence its metabolism. Knowledge of this is important in planning on how to use nicotine and the impact it can have on your body.

## How Nicotine Enters and Affects the Body

The nicotine that comes with smoking or vaping or any other similar activities can easily and quickly get into your bloodstream. In the bloodstream nicotine travels to the brain to interact initially with nicotinic receptors and indirectly with dopamine receptors that release dopamine, an essential neurotransmitter associated with positive reinforcement often related to nicotine use.

Nicotine effects the brain and nervous system which is why its addictive effects are immediate. However, after nicotine has been absorbed through the body it has to be metabolised and expelled out of the system. The time it remains in the system can be influenced by one factor or a combination of the following factors.

## Nicotine Metabolites: Cotinine and Their Role

Nicotine when administered into the body is metabolized in the liver to several products with cotinine being the most famous of all. Cotinine remains in the body for a long time than nicotine because of its longer elimination half-lives. radioactive nicotine usually leaves the body after a few hours, however cotinine stays in the urine for 3-4 days since nicotine was taken. Cotinine may stay in the body for about up to two weeks if one uses tobacco products frequently or take large quantities of nicotine.

**Cotinine** is commonly used in tests to detect nicotine use because it is easier to measure and has a longer half-life. However, it must be noted that, while nicotine, for example, is metabolized and gets out of the body comparatively soon, it is a different case with cotinine since its presence in the system of a person, who is a regular user of nicotine would reveal for a longer period.

### Factors That Affect How Long Nicotine Stays in the System

When it comes to the amount of time nicotine will circulate in your system there are several varying factors. These include:

#### 1. Frequency of Nicotine Use

According to the researchers, one of the main determinants of how long nicotine will remain in the body is if one is a normal or chronic user of nicotine. Daily smokers and vapers may have greater nicotine and its metabolites in their body as their organs may take a longer time to flush out nicotine.
On average, nicotine takes about 2–5 days to clear from the system for frequent smokers while the same takes 1–3 days for occasional smokers who use nicotine products less often.

#### 2. Metabolism Rate

The speed at which nicotine is broken down and removed from the body depends with the metabolic rate of an individual. Some individuals are more metabolic than others, so they will be able to expunge nicotine from body faster than the others who are less metabolic. Hormonal balance is determined by genealogy, age, food intake and much more other factors.

#### 3. Hydration and Body Fat

This is also true by levels of hydration and body fat especially when determining the duration that nicotine stays in the system. [**Nicotine**](/how-long-does-nicotine-stay-in-the-system/)dissolves in water, therefore, if a smoker takes enough water during the smoking process it will be flushed out body. In contrast, the lean people may not store nicotine in their fat tissues as the latter and this make it stay in the body for a longer time.

#### 4. Liver Function

As nicotine passes through the liver, the condition and efficiency of the liver plays a major role in the duration nicotine will take to clear out from the body. Patients with liver disease or poor liver function may slow down the capacity of their liver to metabolite nicotine and thus nicotine takes a longer time to be expelled from the body.

### How Long Does Nicotine Stay in Different Types of Tests?

Duration of time for which nicotine will remain within your bloodstream depends with the specific test that will be conducted. Here’s an overview of how long nicotine and its metabolites remain detectable in different tests:

#### 1. Urine Test

Among the identified urine biomarkers of smoking are cotinine and other metabolites; they remain detectable for 3-4 days in occasional smokers. In the regular smokers Cotinine can be detected for up to two weeks or even more than that.

#### 2. Blood Test

Nicotine is usually detectable in the blood stream depending on the dosage taken within 1-3 days. But the fact is that blood tests to detect nicotine is rare and it is normally used only where the test is designed to check nicotine is present in the blood or not.

#### 3. Saliva Test

Nicotine and all of its metabolites are all detectable in saliva and they can all be detected up to 4 days. This is much shorter than other types of tests, which are usually conducted over a larger amount of time.

#### 4. Hair Test

Hair follicle tests have the capability to show nicotine use for far longer than the 30 days we see with mouth swab or urine tests, in fact up to 90 days or longer. Because hair grows slowly, the results of these tests give a long-term perspective of nicotine use.

**FAQ’s**

1. How long does nicotine stay in your body after smoking?

Nicotine typically stays in your system for 1-3 days after smoking. However, its metabolite, **cotinine**, can be detected in urine for up to 3-4 days for occasional smokers, and up to two weeks for regular smokers.

2. How long does nicotine stay in your system after vaping?

Nicotine from vaping stays in the system for a similar amount of time as smoking, generally 1-3 days. The exact duration depends on factors like the amount of nicotine consumed and your metabolism.

3. What is the half-life of nicotine?

The **half-life** of nicotine is approximately 2 hours. This means that it takes about two hours for the body to eliminate half of the nicotine consumed.

4. Can nicotine be detected in a blood test?

Yes, nicotine can be detected in the blood for up to 3 days after consumption. Blood tests are generally used to detect recent nicotine use.

5. Can nicotine be detected in a urine test?

Yes, nicotine and its metabolite **cotinine** can be detected in urine for up to 3-4 days for occasional smokers, and up to 2 weeks for heavy smokers.

6. How long does nicotine stay in hair follicles?

Nicotine can remain detectable in hair for up to 90 days or even longer, as hair follicle tests can show long-term nicotine use.
